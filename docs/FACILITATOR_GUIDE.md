# Facilitator Guide

Status: early paper-prototype guide.

## What The Exercise Teaches

Incident Bridge helps participants practise:

- Connecting business and technical warning signs.
- Verifying identity before sensitive actions.
- Preserving useful evidence.
- Escalating appropriately.
- Coordinating HR and IT Helpdesk decisions.
- Balancing containment, evidence, continuity, and trust.

Use generic wording: "Follow your organisation's approved reporting, verification, and incident-response procedures."

## Equipment For Paper Prototype

- Shared round cards.
- HR private cards.
- IT Helpdesk private cards.
- Choice sheet for each role.
- Metrics sheet.
- Decision timeline sheet.
- Timer.

## Opening Script

This is a fictional training exercise. It does not use company data, real incidents, or official policy. The goal is to practise discussion and judgement when different roles have different information.

## Running A Round

1. Read the shared incident update.
2. Give HR participants their HR-only information.
3. Give IT Helpdesk participants their IT-only information.
4. Allow brief discussion.
5. Ask every participant to vote for one action.
6. Count votes by role.
7. If a role ties, pause for discussion and choose the final role action.
8. Apply base effects and matching interaction rules.
9. Reveal the public consequence.
10. Ask one or two debrief questions.
11. Record department decisions, metric changes, and flags.

## Timing

Quick mode:

- Rounds 1, 3, and 5.
- Target 8 to 12 minutes.
- Keep discussion tight.

Standard mode:

- Rounds 1 through 5.
- Target 15 to 20 minutes.
- Allow more discussion before locking votes.

## Handling Group Sizes

Three participants:

- One facilitator.
- One HR participant.
- One IT Helpdesk participant.
- Each participant's vote becomes that role's department action.

Six participants:

- Aim for roughly balanced roles.
- Use majority vote by role.
- Resolve ties through facilitator-led discussion.

Approximately fifteen participants:

- Uneven role splits are allowed.
- Watch for one role dominating the room.
- Keep role-private cards concise and voting time visible.

## Handling Problems

Empty role: warn the room and invite volunteers to switch. If needed, the facilitator may proceed for a paper test, but the discussion will be weaker.

Tie: ask the tied department to explain the strongest argument for each tied action, then select the final department action.

Confusion about official policy: remind participants this is generic practice and they should follow approved organisational procedures.

Excessive reading: summarise the shared update aloud and keep the round moving.

## Closing Debrief

Ask:

- Which role had information the other role needed?
- Which trade-off was hardest?
- Where did the group preserve evidence before cleanup?
- Where did speed help, and where did it create risk?
- What should participants check in their actual approved procedures after the exercise?
