2 · Assumptions & Conflicts

CONFLICT — participant count
The master brief text says 3–15 participants. PRODUCT_SPEC.md (higher priority) says 2–9. Resolved in favor of PRODUCT_SPEC: designs and copy target 2–9 participants across 2 roles.
CONFLICT — projector/public view
PROJECT_CONSTRAINTS.md states v1 does not need a projector-specific view; PRODUCT_SPEC lists it under Future Possibilities. The brief's own sections 6, 16 and 18 ask for a full public/projector flow and screens (D01–D04) in this phase. Conservative resolution: I include the public/projector flow map and screens as a labelled future/optional capability — designed now since the brief requests it for review, not treated as committed v1 scope.
Assumption — fictional organisation
Using the master prompt's own example data (fictional org "Northstar Services", employee "Jordan Patel", sender "Maya Chen — People Operations", .example domains) since no source above it supplies names and none conflicts with it.
Assumption — round 1 IT ticket content
The brief's example ticket (§10) shows a full round-2-level compromise timeline. Per GAME_DESIGN's round-by-round reveal, the Round 1 IT ticket in these mockups is scoped down to just the password-reset + unfamiliar-browser signal, matching what GAME_DESIGN actually discloses in round 1.
Assumption — screen IDs
Keeping the brief's P00–P09 / F00–F13 / D01–D04 naming exactly as given, since it doesn't conflict with any higher-priority source and gives every state a stable reference for later phases.

5 · Three Visual Directions

All three keep the DESIGN_SPEC.md colour mandates: amber for active incident, coral for HR, cool blue for IT Helpdesk, green/red reserved for confirmed outcomes and genuine warnings, monospace only for codes/timestamps. They differ in structure, density and mood. Typeface throughout: IBM Plex Sans + IBM Plex Mono.

A · Operational Dark

dark participant / light facilitator

Participant screens use a deep neutral, near-black surface — the phone reads like a live incident channel, amber threading through progress and status. The facilitator switches to a bright, structured control-room surface built for reading at a glance while speaking.

Strengths: strong "this is a live incident" feeling; the dark/light split cleanly separates player experience from host tooling; amber reads clearly against dark.
Risks: dark UI needs careful contrast tuning for outdoor/bright-room phone use; two different base surfaces means twice the surface tokens to maintain.

B · Unified Enterprise Workspace

one high-contrast surface, both roles

Participant and facilitator share the exact same light, high-contrast, black-bordered surface language — dense, modular, SaaS-like. Nothing about the palette shifts between roles; only layout density and control count change. Feels like one coherent internal tool used by everyone in the room.

Strengths: single design language to build and maintain; strong legibility and structure; scales cleanly to dense facilitator screens.
Risks: can read closer to a generic admin tool if borders/weights aren't handled with restraint; less "incident room" atmosphere on the phone.

C · Editorial Incident Briefing

document-like, calm, warm paper tone

A warm, paper-toned surface used by both roles. Artefacts are framed as labelled exhibits rather than app cards; chrome is minimal — thin rules, restrained buttons — and generous whitespace slows the read down deliberately, in keeping with a briefing document rather than a dashboard.

Strengths: distinctive and calm — matches "serious but not alarmist"; the exhibit framing makes artefacts feel more like real evidence, less like generic UI cards.
Risks: lower visual urgency could undersell time pressure in a live room; needs disciplined typography to stay crisp rather than precious, and controls need care to stay obviously tappable/clickable despite minimal chrome.

7 · Recommendation

Recommended: A · Operational Dark. It matches DESIGN_SPEC.md's own stated direction most closely (deep neutral participant base, lighter facilitator surface, amber for active incident), gives participants the strongest "this is live" read on a phone in a room full of people voting at once, and gives the facilitator a bright, legible control surface built for glancing while talking.

C is a close second if a calmer, more distinctive portfolio piece matters more than incident urgency — it's the strongest for realistic artefact presentation. B is the safest, cheapest build if unifying every screen under one visual system outweighs atmosphere.

All three carry forward unchanged into Phase 2 regardless of pick — reply with A, B, or C (or a mix, e.g. "A's participant screens with C's artefact framing").
